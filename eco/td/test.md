## 2. Point-by-Point Feedback & Conceptual Sharpening                                                                               

### A. "Capitalism & Market Economy: The 'social' definition by B & C (Braudel). Definition exploded by the third text."            

  • Very strong opening concept: Mentioning Fernand Braudel's distinction is crucial:                                                  
      • Market economy = Transparent, horizontal, decentralized daily trade.                                                           
      • Capitalism = Opaque, monopolistic, high-level accumulation of capital and power.                                               
  • The paradox to emphasize: Boltanski & Chiapello (B & C) define capitalism through two minimum criteria: unlimited accumulation AND 
  wage-earning subordination (le salariat).                                                                                            
  • Why the 3rd text "explodes" this: Sarah Abdelnour shows that modern platform capitalism and the auto-entrepreneur status           
  deliberately organize a "contournement du salariat" (bypassing wage labor). Capitalism continues to extract surplus value and command
  labor, but without the legal contract of wage labor.                                                                                 

### B. "Why choose an amoral system? / Justifications of capitalism"                                                                

  • The Weberian & Kantian paradox: In itself, capitalism is amoral and absurd (why dedicate one's life to an endless accumulation of  
  abstract value?).                                                                                                                    
  • Tip for the oral: Use the Kant's dove metaphor cited by B & C:                                                                     
  │ A dove feels the wind's resistance and thinks it could fly faster in a vacuum; yet in a vacuum, without air resistance to support  
  │ its wings, it crashes immediately.                                                                                                 
  │ Similarly, capitalism needs moral constraints and justification (the "air") to survive; in a purely amoral void, it destroys its   
  own                                                                                                                                  
  │ social foundation.                                                                                                                 
                                                                                                                                       
  • Clarify the 3 guarantees required by the "Spirit" to enlist workers:                                                               
      1. Enthusiasm (meaningful, exciting work)                                                                                        
      2. Security (protection for oneself and one's family)                                                                            
      3. Common good (proving private profit serves society, through Boltanski & Thévenot's Cités).                                    

### C. "Social & Artistic Critics of Capitalism"                                                                                    

  • Be precise about how the two critiques function historically:                                                                      
      • Social critique: Focuses on exploitation, inequality, misery, and lack of security.                                            
      • Artistic critique: Focuses on alienation, inauthenticity, standardization, and loss of autonomy.                               
  • The turning point (post-May 68): Management absorbed the artistic critique (offering flexible hours, flatter hierarchies, teamwork,
  casual dress) at the expense of the social critique (dismantling job security, weakening trade unions, outsourcing).                 

### D. "The 3 Spirits of Capitalism" (Refining "the first duh", 2nd, and 3rd)                                                       

  Avoid casual phrasing like "the first duh" in the oral:                                                                              
                                                                                                                                       
  • 1st Spirit (Late 19th c.): Firme patrimoniale et familiale. Hero: the bourgeois entrepreneur. Justification: Cité domestique       
  (paternalism, family lineage) & Cité marchande.                                                                                      
  • 2nd Spirit (1930s–1970s): Firme managériale intégrée (Fordism). Hero: the salaried director and the "cadre". Justification: Cité   
  industrielle (efficiency, planning, DPO) & Cité civique (welfare state, collective bargaining, stable careers).                      
  • 3rd Spirit (1990s+): Firme-réseau (Lean organization). Hero: the nomad consultant, the "entrepreneur of the self". Justification:  
  Cité par projets (mobility, connectivity, employability).                                                                            

### E. "Self-employed entrepreneur / sole trader: '4th Spirit'?"                                                                    

  • Sociological critique: Does auto-entrepreneuriat qualify as a 4th spirit?                                                          
      • Argument AGAINST: According to B & C, a spirit must provide security and a believable common good. Abdelnour demonstrates that 
      auto-entrepreneurship provides neither: it strips workers of safety nets, generates intense economic anxiety, and amounts to     
      "salariat déguisé" (disguised wage labor).                                                                                       
      • Argument FOR (or better, "Paroxysm"): Rather than a brand new spirit, it is the radicalization / weaponization of the 3rd      
      spirit. The myth of the autonomous "entrepreneur of oneself"—initially tailored for elite IT consultants and creative cadres in  
      the 1990s—is now forcibly imposed on the working class (delivery riders, cleaners, subcontracted artisans) as an "injonction à   
      l'autonomie".                                                                                                                    

### F. "Failles" (What to include)                                                                                                  

  Grouping all criticisms into a vague final bullet called "Failles" will weaken your conclusion. Instead, distinguish the two types of
  "failles":                                                                                                                           
                                                                                                                                       
  1. Epistemological & methodological limits of B & C (Françoise Piotet's critique):                                                   
      • Managerialist bias: B & C read management books/manuals and mistook prescriptive discourse for actual shopfloor reality.       
      • Neglect of material constraints: They ignored demographic shifts, technology, and economic forces.                             
      • The blind spot of the State: B & C ignored public regulation.                                                                  
  2. Empirical and institutional realities (Sarah Abdelnour):                                                                          
      • The State is not absent: it actively engineered this deregulation (Loi LME 2008, François Hurel, Hervé Novelli) to break the   
      1945 collective welfare model.                                                                                                   
      • The paradox of autonomy: instead of emancipation, it leads to Foucault's "société de contrôle" (the worker becomes their own   
      boss and their own oppressor, losing Robert Castel's "affranchissement de l'hégémonie du travail").                              

## 3. Recommended Restructured Plans                                                                                                

  Here are two ways to organize your ideas into a standard academic format:                                                            

### Option A: The Standard 3-Part Academic Plan (Recommended for CPES / TD)                                                         

  Matches the classical progression: Theoretical Framework → Contemporary Mutation → Critical Limits & Realities.                      
                                                                                                                                       
  • Introduction:                                                                                                                      
      • Hook: The rise of platform gig work (Uber, Deliveroo) and the revival of independent work.                                     
      • Definitions: Capitalism vs Market Economy (Braudel), the definition of the "firm" and its need for an "esprit".                
      • Problematic: How does the evolution of the firm reflect capitalism's capacity to justify an amoral system through promises of  
      autonomy, to the point of dismantling its own foundational pillar: wage-earning security?                                        
      • Roadmap announcement.                                                                                                          
  • Part I – Le capitalisme et l'impératif de justification : comment susciter l'adhésion à un ordre amoral ?                          
      • A. La nature intrinsèquement amorale du capitalisme et le rôle de "l'esprit" (Braudel vs B & C ; métaphore kantienne de la     
      colombe ; les 3 garanties : enthousiasme, sécurité, bien commun).                                                                
      • B. La dynamique du changement : le troc de la critique artiste contre la critique sociale (Critique artiste vs critique sociale ;
      la récupération managériale post-1968 ; l'érosion des collectifs).                                                               
  • Part II – Des citadelles fordistes à la pulvérisation du salariat : la métamorphose des firmes                                     
      • A. De la firme managériale (2e esprit) à la firme-réseau (3e esprit) (Le compromis fordiste, cadres et DPO → l'entreprise      
      "maigre", la cité par projets, la distinction "Grands" vs "Petits").                                                             
      • B. L'auto-entrepreneur : "4e esprit" ou radicalisation précaire du 3e ? (Extension de "l'entrepreneur de soi" aux classes      
      populaires ; le salariat déguisé et la captation de valeur par les plateformes).                                                 
  • Part III – Les failles du modèle : du discours managérial aux réalités de l'exploitation                                           
      • A. Les failles méthodologiques de B & C relevées par Françoise Piotet (Le biais managérialiste : confondre idéologie des       
      manuels et réalité d'atelier ; l'oubli des contraintes matérielles).                                                             
      • B. L'angle mort de l'État et le mirage de l'autonomie (L'État néolibéral architecte du contournement du salariat [Abdelnour] ; 
      autodiscipline foucaldienne et perte de l'affranchissement du travail [Castel]).
  • Conclusion:
      • Synthesis: The firm went from protecting workers within hierarchical fortresses to atomizing them outside company walls.       
      • Opening: Does the revival of legal reclassifications of gig workers (e.g., EU platform directives, Court of Cassation rulings) 
      signal the return of the social critique?

### Option B: The Dynamic 2-Part Plan (Punchy & Direct for a 10-Minute Talk)

  • Part I – La transformation conjointe des firmes et de l'esprit du capitalisme : la promesse d'autonomie comme outil d'engagement   
      • A. Justifier l'amoral : Définition braudélienne, nécessité d'un esprit, et absorption de la critique artiste au détriment de la

      • B. De la firme hiérarchique à l'auto-emploi : Le passage de la firme managériale (2e esprit) à la firme-réseau (3e esprit),

  • Part II – Les "failles" : de l'illusion managériale à la déconstruction politique du salariat

      • B. La réalité empirique (Abdelnour) : Le rôle clé de l'État dans l'effritement du salariat, le piège du salariat déguisé et la

## 4. Checklist for Delivery

  1. Explicit Signposting: Explicitly announce transitions out loud ("Après avoir analysé la mécanique justificatrice du capitalisme,  
  voyons comment celle-ci s'incarne dans les morphologies successives de la firme...").
  2. Anchor with Authors: Whenever presenting an argument, name the author immediately ("Comme le rappelle Françoise Piotet...", "Sarah
  Abdelnour démontre que...", "En mobilisant Robert Castel...").
  3. Keep the "4th Spirit" as an open rhetorical question: Frame it as: "Peut-on parler d'un quatrième esprit du capitalisme ? En      
  réalité, il s'agit plutôt du paroxysme précarisant du troisième." This shows advanced critical thinking.
